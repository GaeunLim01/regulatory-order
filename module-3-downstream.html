This module applies uniformly regardless of a case's Module 1 or Module 2 classification. A Conflict-born order and an Amendment-born order are transmitted, distributed, and responded to through the same mechanisms. That is why it sits underneath both axes rather than beside them.

3.1

How the rule actually reaches you

Transmission. Order does not reach actors through legal text alone — it travels through classifications, deadlines, contracts, documentation systems, dependencies, access decisions, and enforcement signals.

  • What you get categorized asclassification_decision

    The assigned category becomes the main channel through which order reaches firms and users.

    Ask: what has this object come to be treated as?
  • The real operative deadlinedeadlines

    The operative deadline is often a preparation gate earlier than the formal legal date.

    Ask: which clock actually governs?
  • Passed down through contractscontractual_relations

    Private clauses carry data demands, cost pass-through, and replacement obligations past the legal addressee.

    Ask: how does the rule travel beyond the named party?
  • What you have to prove, and howdocumentation_systems

    The system demanding evidence is where exposure is felt.

    Ask: what must be produced, and who can produce it?
  • Exposure depends on your spot in the chainsupply_chain_dependence

    The same order arrives differently depending on upstream or downstream position.

    Ask: where in the chain does the burden land?
  • Access granted, narrowed, or cut offaccess_restriction

    Order materializes through access decisions.

    Ask: who can still reach the capability?
  • Investigations, penalties, guidanceenforcement_signals

    Make an existing order operationally salient.

    Ask: the rule didn't change — so what became salient?

3.2

Who ends up holding the bag

Distribution. Position and leverage. Source and Bearer are relational positions, not fixed identities — the same actor can be a Source for one order and a Bearer for another.

  • Source (Generator)position

    The actor producing the underlying capability, asset, demand, or regulatory trigger around which order forms.

  • Bearer (Absorber)position

    The actor standing where the order actually produces consequences, often carrying nominal responsibility or immediate exposure.

  • Bargaining power (Leverage)capacity

    The ability to contest, deflect, absorb, or pass on a burden. Frequently shapes the final distribution more than formal position does.

Mechanisms

  • Named party ≠ paying partyburden_displacement

    The party a rule formally names is not necessarily the party that ultimately absorbs the cost or risk.

  • Costs roll downhilldownstream_propagation

    Consequences travel along operational dependencies. Actors with little power over the original dispute inherit the disruption, cost, and legal exposure.

  • Enforcement makes it heavierenforcement_amplification

    Investigations, penalties, and comparable cases intensify the burden attached to an existing order without any change to the legal text.

    Distinct from 3.1's enforcement signals: that concerns how order reaches actors; this concerns how weight already in place gets redistributed.
When several orders operate at once

Can be set aside in single-case diagnosis. A single actor may face several orders on different timetables simultaneously, and exposures that are each manageable in isolation can erode effective leverage once they accumulate.

3.3

How firms reshape the field in response

Re-formation. Firms restructure supply chains, redesign products, rewrite contracts, relocate jurisdictions, lobby, and create new intermediaries. These responses change what is visible, who appears responsible, and where strategic risk sits.

The altered field feeds into the next round of Module 2. This is feedback the actors generate themselves — the only point at which the three modules touch, and it runs forward in time rather than establishing a hierarchy among them.

External shocks that force change · Contextual Amplification

Geopolitical and macroeconomic conditions — war, sanctions realignment, currency shocks, political turnover, financial crisis — sit in the background at low intensity and can raise the stakes attached to an order already in place. Past a threshold, they can trigger re-formation directly. Unlike firms' own adaptation, this route comes from outside the field the order governs.

Why only this module carries numbers

These numeric fields apply to Module 3 alone. Modules 1 and 2 remain purely qualitative — they classify what kind of thing is happening, not how much of it. Forcing a score onto a classification judgment, such as rating how much of a jurisdiction contest a case is, manufactures false precision rather than adding information.
