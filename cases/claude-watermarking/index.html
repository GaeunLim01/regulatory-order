Narrative

Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires providers of generative systems to mark outputs in a machine-readable form, so people can tell content was machine-made. After signing the related Code of Practice, Anthropic began embedding imperceptible text watermarks and provenance metadata in Claude outputs worldwide. The rollout met user criticism and questions about how well the mark holds.

On paper it is simple: the content should carry a detectable signal of its origin. In practice that signal has to survive editing, remixing, and passage through other models, and once content leaves the system that applied it, the stable signal the rule assumes rarely holds.

Three mechanics on this case

Ordering

Article 50 draws the boundary around the output of generative systems, not around weights or compute. The duty is formally tied to the EU market, yet the provider's implementation was global, pushing the boundary's practical reach past its formal perimeter.

Manifestation

The instruments are a statutory duty, a voluntary Code of Practice, and provider-level marking meant to survive light editing. The limits show quickly: reprocessing or passage through an unmarked model can weaken or strip the mark, and short outputs, code, and lightly edited text sit in ambiguity. The gap is between a design that treats marking as a stable signal and how digital content actually circulates.

Distribution

The legal duty falls on the provider. By going global, the company extended the practical burden of marking to all users. The content then travels through platforms, users, and third-party tools, none of them bound to keep the mark intact. So the rule holds one party responsible for a signal that everyone downstream is free to erase.

Where the three meet

The object shifts from chips, remote access, and model weights to the outputs of generative systems. A legislative boundary passes through soft-law guidance and provider choices, then is tested in everyday use. The same gap returns, between a design that treats the mark as stable and the fluid way digital content moves.
