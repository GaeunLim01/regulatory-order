Object held

A transparency mark on AI-generated output — scoped by law to EU-market use, applied here at the model level worldwide.

Where it started to move

Not a change in the statute. One global pipeline cost less to build than a geofenced one.

Where cost arrives first

Engineering and operations chose the cheaper excess. Downstream users inherit a mark that cannot tell generation from light editing.

Difference carried

A duty written for one market became the default everywhere, including the assistive use Article 50(2) exempts.

What Article 50 was actually built to bind

Article 50(2) binds AI systems as used within the EU — nothing in it reaches a response generated for a user in Singapore or São Paulo. Anthropic marked every Claude output everywhere anyway, not because the law required it, but because one global pipeline is cheaper to build than a geofenced one.

WhatWhat actually happened
Article 50(2) was built to bindSystems as used within the EU market, exempting tools performing assistive, light-editing functions that don't substantially alter the user's own input.
Anthropic applied it toEvery Claude output worldwide, at the model level, regardless of where the user is or what they asked for.
The stated reasonEngineering cost — geofencing would mean residency detection and routing logic for every call; one marked pipeline is simpler to ship.

Core Article 50 obligations became applicable on August 2, 2026. Roughly 200 companies, including Microsoft, Google, Meta, and OpenAI, had signed the associated Code of Practice, which carries fines up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover for non-compliance. Anthropic confirmed on August 10 that models launched from August 2 onward would carry the mark globally. Policy critics called the result a "Brussels effect" running one token at a time — EU law setting a default disclosure regime for users it never had authority over.

Who meant what, and what happened instead

A rule scoped to one market became the default everywhere, because matching it exactly would have cost more than exceeding it.

ActorMeant toWhat happened instead
EU legislators Bind AI-content marking to systems used in the EU market, exempting tools that only lightly edit a user's own input. Got a global default disclosure regime for users the law never had authority over, because reaching everyone was cheaper than reaching only the EU.
Anthropic Ship one marking pipeline instead of a geofenced one, avoiding residency-detection and routing costs. Marked text for users who typed their own words through Claude for light editing — exactly the case Article 50(2) exempts, because a statistical watermark can't tell invention from repair.
Policy critics Evaluate whether the marking met the letter of a transparency law. Named it an unforced "Brussels effect," criticizing a choice the law never required as if it were compliance.
Downstream users & platforms Rely on the mark as a signal of AI origin. Anthropic itself warns a detected mark shows only that Claude may have processed the content, not that it wrote it — and that the absence of a mark proves nothing either.
