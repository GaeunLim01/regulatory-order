Narrative

Days after Anthropic released Fable 5 and Mythos 5, the Commerce Department issued a directive requiring that access by any foreign national be suspended, citing the risk of diversion to foreign military-intelligence users. Because nationality could not be verified in real time, the models were switched off for everyone. Access was restored within weeks, in stages, after added safeguards.

The restriction was written around a category that is clear in law but nearly invisible in the running system. When a rule needs an identity check the infrastructure does not have, the only enforceable version is the broadest one.

Three mechanics on this case

Ordering

Export authority was applied to model weights and their accessibility. The boundary was drawn emergently: foreign-national access to these specific models was treated as a controlled transaction on security grounds, without the ordinary prior classification. A category clear in law was asserted over a system that could not see it.

Manifestation

The instrument was an immediate directive to suspend foreign-national access under threat of penalty. With no real-time nationality check, the only compliant action was total suspension. The rule achieved speed at the cost of over-breadth, and exposed how hard a nationality-based boundary is to enforce on a globally reachable, API-accessible model.

Distribution

Authority sat with Commerce. The burden fell at once on the provider, forced to choose between imperfect screening and total suspension. Users, including U.S. persons, lost access without warning. The risk assessment stayed with the government; the operational responsibility for segmenting users in real time was pushed onto the provider.

Where the three meet

A rule written for a few ended up landing on everyone, because the line it drew was one the system could not yet read. The provider absorbed the operational weight, and the limits of the instrument showed almost immediately. This was a compressed loop, the whole cycle in three weeks, not a slow legislative redesign.
