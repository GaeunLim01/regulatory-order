What deemed export was already built to catch

EAR's deemed-export rule already treats giving a foreign national access to controlled technology as an export to that person's home country — no new statute needed, unlike the compute-access gap RASA exists to close. That's what let Commerce act in hours: a letter from the Commerce Secretary to Anthropic's CEO, three days after release, ordering all foreign-national access cut off immediately.

WhatWhat actually happened
Deemed export (existing doctrine)Foreign-national access to controlled technology, in the U.S. or abroad, already counts as an export to that person's nationality — this predates the case.
Applied here toAPI and hosted access to a frontier model's weights and outputs, not a lab visit or a technical briefing.
Why it could move so fastNo legislation needed — Commerce invoked existing authority directly, by letter, the same week the models shipped.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5 on June 9, 2026, with the more capable Mythos 5 limited to Project Glasswing partners. Three days later, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick ordered all foreign-national access to both models suspended, citing a reported jailbreak flagged by Amazon and NSA reviewers. Unable to verify nationality in real time, Anthropic shut both models down for every customer worldwide. Access came back in stages over the following weeks.

Who meant what, and what happened instead

A rule written for a narrow risk ended up landing on everyone, because the line it drew was one the running system couldn't read in real time.

ActorMeant toWhat happened instead
Amazon & NSA reviewers Flag one specific jailbreak vulnerability to Commerce. Triggered a shutdown far broader than the vulnerability they'd actually reported.
Commerce Secretary Lutnick Cut off foreign-national access to that specific risk within days of it being flagged. Because nationality couldn't be checked in real time, took the model down for every customer worldwide, including domestic users and Anthropic's own staff.
Anthropic Comply immediately and keep its other models running unaffected. Absorbed 18 days without its two flagship models, refunded recent subscribers, and became the first company ordered to revoke access to specific model versions by user nationality.
Cybersecurity researchers Use Fable 5 for legitimate defensive security work. Lost access along with everyone else; over 100 researchers publicly objected that the ban was broader than the risk it named.
Commerce (again, late June) Restore access in exchange for Anthropic's coordination commitments — proactive risk reporting, agreed release protocols. Restored it in stages instead of all at once: Mythos 5 to vetted partners first, Fable 5 fully two weeks later.
