Narrative

Anthropic held two uses it would not allow: fully autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance. Not "unless the law requires it." A flat no. The Department sought contract language allowing all lawful purposes. When the company refused, the government moved to supply-chain risk designation and orders to cease use. The episode opened a contest over where the use-boundary should sit, and over the reach of the instruments available to set it.

The Department of Defense remains the legal name. A September 2025 executive order authorized "Department of War" as a secondary title for non-statutory use, the usage this case's short name reflects.

Three lenses on this case

Ordering →

The boundary in contest is permissible downstream use. Anthropic drew it to exclude mass surveillance of Americans and fully autonomous lethal systems; the government wanted a boundary set only by "lawful purposes." This was not two sides negotiating under one authority. Each defined the thing being governed differently: a lawful capability for the mission, or a model whose acceptable uses were still partly the company's to govern.

Manifestation →

When negotiation failed, the government shifted to blunter instruments: supply-chain risk designation, a directive to cease agency use, the threat of Defense Production Act compulsion, then litigation. The tools are powerful but coarse, and they produced legal challenge and the friction of phasing out an already-deployed model. Contract terms could not settle a disagreement about what the thing was in the first place.

Distribution →

Authority to define the use-boundary was claimed on both sides: by the company through acceptable-use policy, by the government through national-security and procurement powers. The risk designation aimed to push pressure past the direct contract into the wider contractor ecosystem. Knowledge of the contemplated end uses sat mostly with government customers; responsibility for downstream misuse stayed in contention.

Where the three meet

The use cases were the surface. Underneath was a quieter question: who gets to define what the model is allowed to be. When a private and a public actor answered it differently, strong but imperfect instruments came out, and their limits showed at once, unresolved in court rather than settled by contract.
