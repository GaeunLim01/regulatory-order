Where the 2009 line stopped reaching

Congress writes ECRA's operative categories — export, reexport, transfer — in general terms; BIS can only rule on whether a new situation fits inside them, not add one of its own. Three advisories in a row could only say remote access didn't fit. Only Congress could write it in as a fourth category, which is exactly what RASA does.

WhatWhat actually counted as regulated
Old reading (2009–2014)BIS's own repeated ruling that remote use doesn't fit inside "export," "reexport," or "transfer" — not a new category, just a negative answer inside the existing ones.
The gapA chip can sit in a data center overseas and still be reached, and used, from anywhere — nothing that fits Congress's existing categories, and nothing BIS alone can add one to cover.
RASA (proposed)Amends ECRA itself to add "remote access" as a fourth category alongside export, reexport, and transfer.

Three separate advisories — 2009, 2011, 2014 — reaffirmed the old reading rather than revisiting it. Only once BIS's own 2023 rule flagged the gap did Congress move: first a bill that stalled in 2024, then a reintroduced version that passed the House 369–22 in January 2026 and now sits with the Senate.

Timeline

January 12, 2026The House passes it again, 369-22; it moves to the Senate as S. 3519, with McCormick, Wyden, Cotton, and Coons among its sponsors there.

Who meant what, and what happened instead

The old reading passed only a light duty into the value chain. When that left too much uncovered, the answer wasn't to reinterpret the boundary — it was to try rewriting it, in statute.

ActorMeant toWhat happened instead
BIS (2009–2023) Keep the 2009 reading stable through advisory opinions, reaffirming it rather than revisiting it. By 2023, had to publicly flag the gap itself in its own rule instead of patching it by advisory again.
Cloud & infrastructure providers Preserve the existing reading — 2024 comment letters argued for keeping the lighter, non-exporter duty they'd operated under for over a decade. If RASA clears the Senate, absorb new licensing, due-diligence, and liability obligations instead — now the party expected to know who's actually using the compute.
Congress (Lawler, McCormick, Wyden, Cotton, Coons) Close the gap by statute, moving the trigger from the chip to the act of accessing it. Passed the bill twice — stalled in 2024, then 369–22 through the House in 2026 — and it's still not law; the old reading remains in force while the Senate sits on S. 3519.
BIS (2026) Close the same gap on its own, by rule, rather than wait on Congress — a gap it helped widen by letting a prior Know-Your-Customer requirement lapse. Drafted the rule anyway, even as its own lawyers reportedly doubt it has enforcement authority without the statute RASA would provide.
