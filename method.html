Framework v1.0

Methodology

Five layers of one question: how does regulatory order form, and how does it reach the firms standing under it?

There is no shortage of work on how firms handle regulation: what motivates compliance, how organizations respond strategically, how law is reworked inside institutions. That work tends to begin once the demand is already legible to the firm. What makes it legible is left as background.

That step is the subject here. A regulator that cannot reach an arrangement does not always move its boundary; sometimes it reaches past the boundary, through what a provider knew, or through a fact it produces administratively because the world does not supply one. Firms, for their part, often meet a rule first as a contract clause or a documentation request rather than as legal text. Both leave marks in the documentary record, and the five layers below are the procedure for reading them.

Where the procedure came from

Worked out across three working papers, each reading a regulatory sequence in full. The layers are that reading set out on its own, so it can be applied to a case other than the ones it was built on.

  • The Cloud Compute Question in U.S. Export Controls, 2009-2026. How one regime answered the same question six times, and what changed when it stopped redrawing lines and added a category.
  • When Governance Cannot Hold Its Object. What happens to location, timing, and definitional authority when governance tries to constitute AI as something it can regulate.
  • How Regulation Becomes Firm-Level Exposure. The pathways by which a rule turns into pressure a particular firm can feel, and why exposure and the cost of servicing it are separate quantities.
Scope

Applies to regulatory orders that national or international authorities currently form or operate through documented rules, formal classifications, and administratively recognizable decisions. Tracing is confined to the documentary record: published rules, official gazettes, consultation records, court filings, industry comments.

A Classification: why is the object unstable

A.1 Object type

What kind of thing is being regulated. Select one.

CapabilityPhysical and informational layers are inseparably combined. Each layer is legible alone; the combined regulatory reading splits.
Access & dataAccess, use, transmission, and generated output are the key events. Identity, jurisdiction, and definitional authority are all entangled.
RelationalThe tie between parties is itself the regulated object: contracts, equity stakes, supply agreements, access arrangements. Parties are identifiable; the scope of the tie and its downstream inheritance rarely settle.
Physical goodsIdentity is comparatively fixed (chips, steel); movement is the key event. Jurisdiction and definitional authority stay separable.

A.2 Instability conditions

Conditions under which the object resists stable definition. Select all that apply. These hold independently of whether any dispute occurred, which is Layer B's question, so this section is worked through on every case.

Jurisdictional overlapTwo or more authorities can each claim definitional authority over the same object. Whether they actually did is recorded in Layer B.
Speed mismatchProcedure and the object run on different cycles. Persists even when every party's purpose aligns.
Category-practice mismatchThe regulatory category and the actual pattern of use do not line up: the defined class captures activity its addressees do not recognize as belonging to it.

B Formation: how the boundary reached its current shape

Traced backward through the documentary record. Independent of Layer A: an Enactment can still carry a full set of instability conditions.

B.1 Event type

A two-step branch, not a flat three-way choice.

Step 1. Is there a prior state to compare against?
  • No → Enactment Obligations arise whole. No accumulated cases, so Step 2 and the timeline do not apply.
  • Yes → go to Step 2
Step 2. Did actors come into direct opposition with one another?
  • Yes → Dispute Reactive.
  • No → Amendment Proactive. No direct opposition, but the amendment creates a burden for other actors later.
Event typeWhat gets marked on the timeline
DisputePoints where actors came into direct opposition.
AmendmentThe path along which burden arises after the amendment.
EnactmentNot applicable. There is no prior timeline.

B.2 Reference corpus

U.S. BIS computing and semiconductor export-control rules, 2009 → 2026 Remote Access Security Act. This is a trajectory, not a case. Individual amendments are cut from it and registered as cases.

D Distribution: who ends up holding it

Position and leverage. Source and Bearer are relational positions rather than fixed identities. The same actor can be a Source for one order and a Bearer for another, sometimes within a single order.

TermKindDefinition
SourcePositionThe actor producing the underlying capability, asset, demand, or regulatory trigger around which order forms.
BearerPositionThe actor standing where the order actually produces consequences.
Bearer typeAttributefirm identifiable, nameable.  diffuse an unbounded population of individuals that cannot be enumerated.
LeverageCapacityThe ability to contest, deflect, absorb, or pass on a burden. Frequently shapes the final distribution more than formal position does.
MechanismMeaning
Burden displacementThe party a rule formally names is not the party that absorbs the cost or risk.
Downstream propagationConsequences travel along operational dependencies; actors with no power over the original dispute inherit the disruption, cost, and exposure.
Enforcement amplificationInvestigations, penalties, and comparable-case citations intensify the burden attached to an existing order without any change to the legal text.

E Re-formation: what happens next

Firms restructure supply chains, redesign products, rewrite contracts, relocate jurisdictions, lobby, and create new intermediaries. These responses change what is visible, who appears responsible, and where strategic risk sits. The altered field feeds into the next round of Layer B.

Contextual amplification: geopolitical and macroeconomic conditions (war, sanctions realignment, currency shocks, political turnover, financial crisis) sit in the background at low intensity and can raise the stakes attached to an order already in place. Past a threshold they trigger re-formation directly.
