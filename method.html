Framework v5

Methodology

Five layers of one question: how does regulatory order form, and how does it reach the actors standing under it?

Scope

Applies to regulatory orders that national or international authorities currently form or operate through documented rules, formal classifications, and administratively recognizable decisions. Tracing is confined to the documentary record: published rules, official gazettes, consultation records, court filings, industry comments. Exchanges that never entered that record are out of reach — this does not mean such paths don't exist, only that this method cannot see them.

Layer A reads conditions; Layer B reads events. A condition holds whether or not anyone came into opposition. An event requires that something happened. Every case carries values on both, so a case can be an Enactment (Layer B) and still show a full set of instability conditions (Layer A) — this is the design, not an anomaly. Layer C is downstream of both A and B. Layer D and Layer E apply uniformly once an order exists, regardless of what produced it.

A Classification — why is the object unstable

A.1 Object type

What kind of thing is being regulated. Select one.

CapabilityPhysical and informational layers are inseparably combined. Each layer is legible alone; the combined regulatory reading splits.
Access & dataAccess, use, transmission, and generated output are the key events. Identity, jurisdiction, and definitional authority are all entangled.
RelationalThe tie between parties is itself the regulated object: contracts, equity stakes, supply agreements, access arrangements. Parties are identifiable; the scope of the tie and its downstream inheritance rarely settle.
Types not yet exercised by the current case set
Physical goodsIdentity is comparatively fixed (chips, steel); movement is the key event. Jurisdiction and definitional authority stay separable.

A.2 Instability conditions

Conditions under which the object resists stable definition. Select all that apply. These hold independently of whether any dispute occurred, which is Layer B's question, so this section is worked through on every case. A case can still come out with none, in which case say why rather than leaving it silent.

Jurisdictional overlapTwo or more authorities can each claim definitional authority over the same object. Whether they actually did is recorded in Layer B.
Speed mismatchProcedure and the object run on different cycles. Persists even when every party's purpose aligns.
Category-practice mismatchThe regulatory category and the actual pattern of use do not line up: the defined class captures activity its addressees do not recognize as belonging to it.

When recording jurisdictional overlap or speed mismatch, write out both sides. In the semiconductor corpus one side was near-constant (U.S. jurisdiction, or the legislative cycle), which made it tempting to record only the item name. That shortcut does not survive contact with orders formed elsewhere. Where a case has more than two claiming parties, or where one party's legal hook attaches to something other than the object, record that rather than forcing it into a pair.

Object opacity — a candidate fourth condition, not yet added

Raised twice independently (Anthropic v. DoW, Fable 5/Mythos 5), both turning on what can be known about a model from outside it. Two appearances meets the bar for a batch review, but it is not in the controlled vocabulary yet — recorded as a flagged new value on the cases that show it (R6).

Layer B · Formation →

B Formation — how the boundary reached its current shape

Traced backward through the documentary record. Independent of Layer A: an Enactment can still carry a full set of instability conditions (see the EU AI Act watermarking case in the worked examples below).

B.1 Event type

A two-step branch, not a flat three-way choice.

Step 1. Is there a prior state to compare against?
  • No → Enactment Obligations arise whole. No accumulated cases, so Step 2 and the timeline do not apply.
  • Yes → go to Step 2
Step 2. Did actors come into direct opposition with one another?
  • Yes → Dispute Reactive.
  • No → Amendment Proactive. No direct opposition, but the amendment creates a burden for other actors later.
Event typeWhat gets marked on the timeline
DisputePoints where actors came into direct opposition.
AmendmentThe path along which burden arises after the amendment.
EnactmentNot applicable. There is no prior timeline.

The point at which the order began operating is also marked. Enactment's blank timeline is not missing data: it exits the branch at Step 1, before the question applies.

B.2 Reference corpus

U.S. BIS computing and semiconductor export-control rules, 2009 → 2026 Remote Access Security Act. This is a trajectory, not a case. Individual amendments are cut from it and registered as cases.

Layer C · Manifestation →

C Manifestation — how it shows up in the record

Merged in v5

v4 split this across two modules: "firm-level traces" under the object's classification, and "transmission channels" under downstream observation. Across all three registered cases the two lists fired together every time and never once separated — 3 for 3. Two names for one phenomenon, so v5 merges them into a single checklist, held downstream of both Layer A and Layer B: which of the two produced a given footprint is not what this layer answers.

What instability and formation actually look like once they leave a trace in filings, statements, contracts, or reporting. Select all that apply; most cases will show several.

Boundary driftThe legal text looks stable, yet who falls under the rule keeps shifting.
Definitional contestSeveral actors apply different working definitions to the same object at once.
Competing clocksFixed-cycle processes such as contract dates and effective dates run alongside continuous ones like ongoing access, use, and volume.
Recurring resetA fixed-cycle process such as an annual review or delegated legislation keeps resetting the threshold itself.

DeadlinesWhich clock actually governs?
Contractual relationsHow does the rule travel beyond the named party?
Documentation systemsWhat must be produced, and who can produce it?
Supply-chain positionWhere in the chain does the burden land?
Access restrictionWho can still reach the capability?

Enforcement is not listed here. It redistributes weight that is already in place rather than introducing an order, so it sits in Layer D.

Layer D · Distribution →

D Distribution — who ends up holding it

Position and leverage. Source and Bearer are relational positions rather than fixed identities. The same actor can be a Source for one order and a Bearer for another, sometimes within a single order. Applies uniformly regardless of Layer A or Layer B: burden displacement and downstream propagation show up whether the object type is Capability, Relational, or Access & data.

TermKindDefinition
SourcePositionThe actor producing the underlying capability, asset, demand, or regulatory trigger around which order forms.
BearerPositionThe actor standing where the order actually produces consequences.
Bearer typeAttributefirm identifiable, nameable.  diffuse an unbounded population of individuals that cannot be enumerated.
LeverageCapacityThe ability to contest, deflect, absorb, or pass on a burden. Frequently shapes the final distribution more than formal position does.
MechanismMeaning
Burden displacementThe party a rule formally names is not the party that absorbs the cost or risk.
Downstream propagationConsequences travel along operational dependencies; actors with no power over the original dispute inherit the disruption, cost, and exposure.
Enforcement amplificationInvestigations, penalties, and comparable-case citations intensify the burden attached to an existing order without any change to the legal text.
Concurrent orders

A single actor may face several orders on different timetables at once. Exposures each manageable in isolation can erode effective leverage once they accumulate. Can be set aside in single-case diagnosis.

Layer E · Re-formation →

E Re-formation — what happens next

Firms restructure supply chains, redesign products, rewrite contracts, relocate jurisdictions, lobby, and create new intermediaries. These responses change what is visible, who appears responsible, and where strategic risk sits. The altered field feeds into the next round of Layer B. This is feedback the actors generate themselves.

Contextual amplification. Geopolitical and macroeconomic conditions (war, sanctions realignment, currency shocks, political turnover, financial crisis) sit in the background at low intensity and can raise the stakes attached to an order already in place. Past a threshold they trigger re-formation directly. Unlike firms' own adaptation, this route comes from outside the field the order governs.

A field that needs a judgment about where to stop counting ends up measuring how much was reported rather than what happened. The scope limit here is documentary, so that judgment is unavoidable once a field scales with coverage.

Layer A and Layer B carry no numeric fields and none may be added. Scoring a classification judgment manufactures precision rather than adding information.
