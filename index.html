AI GOVERNANCE × REGULATORY ORDER

Regulation has to decide what it is even holding.

When a new technology becomes a matter of national security, I read recent cases for where that grip holds and where it slips.

Five cases, meant as a sample of the pattern below, not a running list of every case that fits it. They sit in the U.S. and the EU because that is where this kind of friction has been loudest, not because the pattern only works there.

What that reading answers

Object held

The rule has to pick a thing. A chip. Remote access. A nationality. A file.

Where it started to move

That thing sat outside the categories on the books. Not overlooked — not written yet.

Where cost arrives first

Legal, operations, procurement, or one named person. Rarely the desk the statute pointed at.

Difference carried

A gap someone else designed, held by the firm anyway. Sometimes by an employee instead.

How I read a case

Most accounts of a case stop at the outcome — a ban, a ruling, a seizure. Reading past that leaves a few questions standing, whatever the case. Not stages of a shared model, just what's left once the outcome stops being the interesting part.

Who designed the categories this rests on, and who only gets to interpret them?

Someone writes the categories in general terms. Usually a legislature, and usually before this situation existed: export, reexport, transfer; a designation built for one kind of vendor; a transparency duty scoped to one market.

Someone else only gets to say whether a new case fits. An agency, a regulator, a company reading its own obligations. Which side is holding the pen when the technology shows up is the first thing to check.

Where did that split leave a gap nobody had designed for?

New technology sits outside what was written. Not because anyone missed it. You cannot design a category for a thing that does not exist yet.

A chip you can reach without moving it. Weights that might need policing on their own. An access request no statute named. Those are the gaps worth the page.

What did each actor do into that gap — and who ended up carrying the difference?

This is where a case actually lives. An agency races ahead of its authority. A company ships the cheaper global fix. Someone routes a shipment through the seam between two definitions.

Almost none of them land what they aimed for. The rule built for a company catches an employee. A foreign-adversary tool lands on a domestic dispute. A one-market label becomes the default everywhere. Somebody holds a difference they did not sign up for.
